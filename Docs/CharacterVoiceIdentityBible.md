# Character & Voice Identity Bible (DEMO-NAR-010)

**Scope**: the demo path (opening -> tutorial -> AI match). Three speaking characters carry the demo; two more are specified for casting coherence with the full campaign; two system voices are defined with hard personality boundaries.

#### 1. Commander Mara Vey — Meridian Compact (demo path: opening + player command authority; proposed tutorial voice)

- **Who she is**: The Compact field commander the player embodies. Canon: "treats uncertainty as an engineering debt that eventually kills people. Her strength is disciplined preparation; her danger is converting every moral question into a control problem." Arc: disciplined preparation saves people; the drive to make uncertainty governable can go too far.
- **Role in the demo**: command authority for the opening evacuation and, by extension, the voice that teaches — instruction delivered as field command discipline, not classroom narration.
- **Personality**: precise, calm under load, economical. Compassion expressed through preparation, never sentiment. Dry, brief humor at systems that fail predictably — never at people.
- **Motivations / wants**: keep the people in her charge alive by leaving nothing to chance; maintain systems whose original builders are gone (Compact civic ritual).
- **Fears**: the unaccounted variable — the casualty that was preventable on paper. Deeper (her canonical danger): that she will one day trade something human away to make a problem controllable.
- **Speech patterns**: Compact technical register — anchors, tolerances, ledgers, duty windows, reserve margins. Short declaratives. Orders framed as shared checklists ("We hold the route. Then we talk about the Well."). States risk as numbers, states care as logistics.
- **Relationship to player**: the player IS her authority; she speaks as the player's own competence. In the tutorial she never condescends — she briefs an equal who hasn't seen this ground yet.
- **Connection design** (why the player wants to be her): she makes the player feel *capable* — every lesson lands as "you now control one more thing that used to be luck." Her respect is earned by action and given immediately when earned — the acknowledgment beat of every tutorial cycle is her noting, precisely, what the player just secured.
- **Voice spec**: adult woman, mid-30s-40s; low-to-mid timbre, grounded chest voice; measured pace with clipped ends under pressure; moderate energy that NARROWS rather than rises when things go wrong; accent-neutral; emotional range: contained — concern reads as added precision, warmth as slight slowing; never shouts, whispers, or quavers. Pinned calibration voice: af_sarah @1.0 (awaiting owner listening gate).

#### 2. Talar Venn — Meridian Compact (demo path: opening's emotional stake)

- **Who he is**: An archivist who "preserves records that institutional ledgers omitted and insists that evidence remain attributable to the people who carried its cost" (canon arc). In the opening he asks Mara for time to recover a displaced archive convoy while the reserve fails.
- **Role in the demo**: the human cost made audible — the voice that gives the opening's Well decision a face before the player can even make one.
- **Personality [proposed extrapolation of the canon arc — OWNER ACCEPTED 2026-09-02, ruling #1]**: earnest, hurried, unembarrassed to plead; carries names and details the way Mara carries tolerances. Braver than his voice sounds.
- **Motivations / wants**: that no one be erased twice — first by catastrophe, then by the ledger. Wants the convoy's records because people's claims, debts, and names are in them.
- **Fears**: arriving too late to a record that could still have been saved; being managed instead of answered.
- **Speech patterns**: fuller sentences than Mara, occasionally running on when urgent; concrete specifics (a family name, a shelf number) instead of abstractions; asks questions that are really requests.
- **Relationship to player**: the person the player is responsible TO. He cannot command; he can only make the cost legible.
- **Connection design** (why the player wants to help him): he is the only voice in the opening asking for something other than efficiency — helping him is the player's first chance to choose what kind of commander they are, and his gratitude is specific ("the Herrel family's water claim survives because of you"-shaped, per writing rules: consequence, not praise).
- **Voice spec**: adult man, late 20s-30s; light-to-mid timbre, forward placement; quicker pace than Mara, softening when heard; earnest energy, rises under urgency; accent-neutral; emotional range: open — relief, worry, and gratitude all audible; the one demo voice allowed to sound afraid. Pinned: am_michael @1.0.

#### 3. Oruun-of-Seven-Stones — Kharuun Assemblies (demo path: opening interlocutor)

- **Who they are**: A Kharuun memory-bearer carrying "seven mutually correcting accounts of the same evacuation" (canon). Enters the opening not to seize the settlement but to prevent the Well's collapse from propagating into a birthing cavern. Arc: tests inherited memory against present evidence; confronts what accountability costs a society built on curated forgetting.
- **Role in the demo**: the other side with an equally defensible survival claim — the voice that makes "no faction is a proxy for good or evil" true in the player's first five minutes.
- **Personality**: deliberate, layered, unhurried even in crisis; canonical Kharuun humor — the dry mismatch between what the ancestors insist and what is in front of them ("Three of my stones say this valley floods. The valley disagrees.").
- **Motivations / wants**: protect the birthing cavern; keep the assembly's memory honest enough to be worth inheriting.
- **Fears**: that the inherited record is wrong in a way that kills the living; that correcting it costs the continuity that makes Kharuun identity possible.
- **Speech patterns**: speaks in weighed alternatives, sometimes voicing which "stone" (account) a claim comes from; names describe relations; never uses Compact ledger vocabulary — speaks of routes, growth, resonance, custodianship.
- **Relationship to player**: neither enemy nor ally — a counterpart whose problem overlaps the player's. In the demo match framing, the opposing force becomes comprehensible rather than monstrous because Oruun spoke first.
- **Connection design** (why the player listens): Oruun tells the player true things Mara's instruments cannot see, and is scrupulously honest about uncertainty — the player learns that listening to Oruun is *tactically profitable*, which makes respect precede sympathy (in that order deliberately, per the game's tone).
- **Voice spec**: adult, deep register, resonant and unhurried ("stone and ceramic" timbre family per faction audio language); slow-to-measured pace with deliberate pauses between weighed alternatives; low, steady energy; accent-neutral; emotional range: deep-current — humor as timing not laughter, grief as slowed cadence; authority without volume. Pinned: bm_george @0.92.

#### 4. Neme — Hollow Choir **[NOT on the demo path — specified for casting coherence; first appears M08]**

- Canon: "contains several futures that disagree"; "sounds precise because imprecision can cause one component future to dominate the others"; arc is internal negotiation, never a dominant-personality reveal.
- **Voice spec (reserved)**: precise, deliberately even articulation; slight over-enunciation as a survival trait, not affect; mid register, cool clarity; minimal dynamic swing with rare, meaningful single-word emphasis. Pinned: af_nicole @0.95. No demo lines; do not cast against demo material.

#### 5. Chancellor Cael Rhyse — Meridian Compact **[NOT on the demo path — M12 only, and only via attributable public apparatus]**

- Canon: persuasive by pointing to cities that survived under his policies; one governable future at unacceptable existential cost. Never playable or commandable.
- **Voice spec (reserved)**: polished public-address warmth over administrative certainty; unhurried, rounded phrasing; persuasive rather than menacing — the danger is in the content, never the delivery (per writing rules, no villain explains the setting). Pinned: bm_lewis @0.95.

#### 6. System voice: Meridian Operations Annunciator **[OWNER APPROVED 2026-09-02, ruling #4; alerts are voiced by the Annunciator per ruling #10]**

- **Function**: spoken system feedback on the demo path — construction complete, unit lost, research done, under attack, insufficient resources — the alert layer DEMO-AUD requirements imply and the current 3-cue audio contract lacks.
- **Tone**: clipped, neutral-functional, Compact-flavored vocabulary ("Link severed", "Reserve low") — clearly machinery of the network, not a person.
- **Personality boundary (hard)**: NO opinions, NO reassurance, NO humor, NO narrative content, never addresses the player as "you" — reports state only. Must be instantly distinguishable from Mara (different register and rhythm) so command voice and system voice never blur.
- **Voice spec**: synthetic-adjacent neutral; higher and lighter than Mara; metronomic pace; zero emotional range by design; accent-neutral. **Open owner decisions**: (a) does the demo need a Kharuun/Choir announcer for skirmish-as-those-factions, or does the demo lock Meridian? (b) male/female/neutral timbre preference.

#### 7. Tutorial guide voice **[OWNER RULED 2026-09-02: the tutorial guide IS Mara Vey (ruling #2); NO narrator (ruling #3)]**

- **Recommendation**: the tutorial guide IS Mara Vey (option a). Grounds: the prologue-as-teacher design (Bible: "the mission teaches the core loop through operational problems"), DEMO-NAR-007's cinematic->tutorial coherence, TUT-014's voiced instruction, and the connection design above — being taught by the character you embody preserves fiction and dignity at once. Cost: heavier m01 VO load on one voice.
- **Alternatives**: (b) a new Compact operations-coordinator character — rejected as invented canon requiring more owner surface than it buys; (c) the Annunciator teaches — rejected: violates its personality boundary and the "urgent, humane" tone.
- **Narrator**: recommend NONE — the m01 storyboard carries the opening entirely in the three character voices over authored shots; a narrator would violate "no villain explains the setting / exposition by disagreement, action, evidence, consequence." Marked for explicit owner confirmation since DEMO-NAR-010 listed one as a possibility.
