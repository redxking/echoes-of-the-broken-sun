Campaign dossier / Planned arc

Fifteen missions.
Four possible endings.

The campaign begins with one evacuation and grows into a struggle over which futures are allowed to exist. Every act changes who the player understands, who can stand together, and what “saving Soryn” actually means.

Implementation boundary: The campaign below is the intended fifteen-mission story. Only Mission 1, What the Ledger Keeps, currently exists as a bounded prototype operation, with one durable Well consequence. Missions 2–15, broad branching, dialogue, cinematics, and final presentation are planned—not playable content.
How the story works

Choices return
as facts, not points.

There is no hidden good-or-evil meter. The campaign is designed to remember who paid for a decision, who benefited, what information was available, and whether the player still had another option.

People

Who made it out?

Evacuations, archives, districts, and migration routes create concrete survivors and losses that later missions can acknowledge.

Ground

What changed?

Harvested, preserved, or reshaped Wells alter routes, intelligence, reserves, and the possible tools available later.

Trust

Who believes you?

Alliance composition follows what each faction witnessed—not a single invisible relationship number.

ACT I / 01–05

Necessary Fires

The war begins as a chain of emergencies. Cities need power, migration routes are failing, and every side believes it is preventing a disaster. By the end of the act, the apparent enemy attacks both factions at once.

01
Prototype operation available in development

What the Ledger Keeps

Mara Vey secures an evacuation outside Lume Reach. Talar Venn asks her to recover a displaced archive convoy, while Oruun warns that collapsing the central Well will reach a Kharuun birthing cavern. The player recovers the archive, selects Harvest, Preserve, or Reshape, and withdraws.

Campaign purpose: Teach gathering, scouting, the Future Well decision, and the idea that mission success is not the same as destroying everything.

02
Planned / Oruun

Seven Accounts of Rain

Oruun defends a migration route while seven inherited accounts disagree about which ground can safely carry the Assembly. Players must scout, compare evidence, and protect a moving population rather than trust one perfect map.

Campaign purpose: Introduce Kharuun movement, vibration intelligence, and memory as a system that can correct itself.

03
Planned / Mara

A City on Reserve

Mara stabilizes an ark-city grid through several distributed objectives. No single defensive line can protect every failing subsystem, so the player must decide where limited repair, logistics, and power buy the most time.

Campaign purpose: Turn Meridian connection into a city-scale responsibility and show why Rhyse’s policies appeal to people.

04
Planned / Oruun

The Unburied Road

Oruun uses mobile infrastructure to follow a route that no current map records. A missing memory shard lies beyond shifting terrain, and recovering it may prove that the Assembly’s gaps are not accidents.

Campaign purpose: Develop Waystone migration, adaptation, and the first evidence of deliberate omission.

05
Planned / Joint operation

Terms of Continuance

A ceasefire creates a narrow chance to stabilize both networks. Apparitions attack Compact and Kharuun positions with knowledge neither side should possess. The agreement fails, but the survivors can no longer explain the war as a simple two-sided conflict.

Campaign purpose: End the opening act by making cooperation necessary before either faction is emotionally ready for it.

ACT II / 06–10

The Cost of One Future

The characters learn that their records are incomplete for different reasons. The Choir becomes understandable, and the player discovers that choosing one stable future may save millions while erasing others who can now speak for themselves.

06
Planned / Talar

Names Without Births

Talar traces census entries for people and neighborhoods that have no surviving location. The player must protect civilians and preserve evidence at the same time; destroying every threat may destroy the record being sought.

Campaign purpose: Turn archives into active objectives and reveal that erased futures contained recognizable lives.

07
Planned / Oruun

A Memory Deliberately Lost

Oruun confronts the Assembly council that curated ancestral memory to prevent another civil conflict. The truth is not a simple betrayal: the omission may have saved the society that now has to judge it.

Campaign purpose: Make accountability and continuity compete without declaring either concern meaningless.

08
Planned / Neme

The Shape Beside Us

Neme guides the force through overlapping local states where more than one route or structure remains possible. The apparent apparitions become people with internal disagreements, needs, and limits.

Campaign purpose: Introduce Choir rules through cooperation and make uncertainty readable rather than mystical.

09
Planned / Mara

Reserve Authority

Several districts fail at once, and Mara must decide where the remaining power goes. The operation turns command into triage: every supported grid is visible, and every unsupported district remains part of the record.

Campaign purpose: Bring Mara’s control problem to its sharpest human scale and carry earlier reserve decisions forward.

10
Planned / Three factions

The Choir at Lume Reach

Compact, Kharuun, and Choir forces contest a Well whose harvest could halt a larger cascade. Every side understands a different part of the risk. The decision can save the city and still make the final crisis worse.

Campaign purpose: Close the act with the first fully shared decision and expose the scale of the Crownfall threat.

ACT III / 11–15

Crownfall

The final act asks what should replace a war built around extraction and omission. Previous choices shape who arrives, which routes still exist, and whether the factions can make a decision together.

11
Planned / Alliance state

No Neutral Ledger

The coalition forms from accumulated decisions rather than a fixed cast. Survivors, lost routes, preserved evidence, and faction trust determine who can contribute and which objectives are possible.

Campaign purpose: Make earlier costs operationally visible and show that refusing to choose was also a choice.

12
Planned / Rhyse

The Future That Won

Chancellor Rhyse demonstrates a stable restoration prototype and the lives it can immediately save. Stopping him means accepting uncertainty; supporting him means deciding that unrealized consciousness may be extinguished for everyone else.

Campaign purpose: Give the restoration ending its strongest evidence instead of reducing Rhyse to a villain.

13
Planned / Oruun

Assembly of the Missing

Oruun brings together records from every faction and exposes responsibility shared across old governments, councils, and survival systems. No one leaves with a clean history.

Campaign purpose: Replace blame between civilizations with specific accountability that does not excuse anyone.

14
Planned / Playable Choir

Several Voices, One Command

The player commands the Hollow Choir and maintains incompatible capabilities long enough to solve a crisis. Every possible state has a visible duration and must resolve, turning Neme’s internal negotiation into strategy.

Campaign purpose: Complete the faction perspective before the player decides the Choir’s fate.

15
Planned / Final operation

The Broken Sun

The final battle is an operation to establish a future, not simply eliminate an enemy base. Available forces, terrain, and solutions reflect what the campaign preserved, consumed, revealed, or abandoned.

Campaign purpose: Resolve the Crownfall through restoration, controlled stabilization, extinguishment, or open evolution.

Four intended ending directions

No ending
erases its cost.

The final report is designed to name concrete consequences and unresolved questions. These are ending directions, not four completed cinematic branches.

Restoration

Commit Soryn to one stable future. Cities gain certainty while unrealized branches lose their claim to continue.

Controlled stabilization

Limit the Crownfall without fully closing it. Survival improves, but authority over future decisions remains concentrated.

Extinguishment

End access to the Wells and their danger. The immediate conflict may stop at the cost of power, knowledge, and possibility.

Open evolution

Allow realized and unrealized states to coexist. It preserves possibility while accepting risks no existing society fully understands.
