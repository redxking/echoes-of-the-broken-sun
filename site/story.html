World archive / Soryn

A world haunted by
roads it never built.

The sun did more than break. It left unrealized futures buried across Soryn—places, inventions, and lives that nearly existed and still refuse to vanish completely.

01 / The Crownfall

When possibility
became matter.

Soryn orbits a field of stellar fragments called the Crownfall. The disaster remembered as the breaking of the sun condensed unrealized chains of cause and effect into mineral-organic Dawnshards.

A Dawnshard is not a crystal with a simple amount of energy inside it. It is a remnant of something that could have happened: a technology that was never invented, a district that was never built, or a life that never reached the present. A city can use one to keep its lights on. A researcher can expose knowledge from a path history did not take. Every use also closes that possibility.

Most people encounter the Crownfall in smaller, unsettling ways. A shadow appears twice at noon. Someone remembers a street that no map contains. A worn tool fits the grip of a hand that never held it. These moments are common enough to have names, but not common enough to be understood.

Dawnshards

Possibility made solid

Used for advanced systems, adaptations, support effects, and powerful research. What looks like fuel may also be the last evidence that another future once had a chance.

Future Wells

Several futures in one place

Large deposits where multiple possibilities remain locally coherent. Wells are power sources, archives, sacred wounds, and strategic objectives all at once.

Crownfall echoes

The ordinary impossible

Duplicated shadows, impossible memories, and tools from roads not taken remind people that reality around them is less settled than it appears.

The missing truth

Some possibilities remember

The Hollow Choir reveals that linked consciousness survived inside certain erased branches—and knows that the realized world continued without it.

02 / A short history

Five ages.
One unfinished record.

No archive holds the whole history. Compact records were standardized for survival, Kharuun memory was deliberately curated, and the Choir remembers events that this world says never occurred.

  1. Before Crownfall

    Soryn supported many city cultures and mineral-organic ecologies. The surviving record is fragmentary, and some of it was edited for political reasons long before the present war.

  2. The First Impact Generations

    People gathered around intact infrastructure and formed ark-cities. Deep impact caverns became nurseries for the early Kharuun assemblies.

  3. The Ledger Peace

    Meridian city-states created a shared system for Dawnshard accounting and mutual defense. It prevented local collapse, but placed control of extraction in fewer hands.

  4. The Quiet Omissions

    Kharuun memory-bearers found gaps in communal history. Compact historians found census references to neighborhoods that no archive or ruin could locate.

  5. The Present War

    A chain of unstable Wells threatens both ark-city reserves and Kharuun birthing caverns. Both factions mobilize to survive. Apparitions blamed for the crisis are the first coherent Choir incursions.

03 / Places and living systems

Soryn is not
an empty battlefield.

Its routes, ruins, organisms, and settlements are part of the strategy. Terrain tells stories, hides information, and changes when a Future Well is consumed or reshaped.

Lume Reach

The city on reserve

An ark-city whose failing power reserve drives the opening evacuation. It represents the Compact’s strongest argument: controlled extraction has kept real people alive.

The Glass Scar

A road split by impact

A narrow basin divided by a fractured transit span. Its central Well and three campaign-shaped crossings now have a first authored environment pass in Unreal.

Shivergrass

A clue before the footstep

Probability-sensitive growth that bends before a possible movement. Careful players can read it as a warning without receiving exact enemy information.

Vaultbacks and pale tides

Life adapted to uncertainty

Vaultbacks carry heat-preserving mineral strata and may become mobile cover. Pale tides move through impact basins after Well disturbances. They are ecological systems, not generic monsters.

04 / The first crisis

The Glass Scar
forces a choice.

The opening conflict gives every side an urgent reason to act. Mara Vey needs power and time for an evacuation. Talar Venn needs an archive convoy recovered. Oruun must keep the Well from collapsing into a Kharuun birthing cavern.

Mara commands the Meridian force outside Lume Reach. The simplest military answer is to seize the central Well, use its Dawn, and destroy the opposition. The situation refuses to stay that simple. The archive contains names and records that may explain the Quiet Omissions. The Kharuun are not attacking to capture the city. They are trying to stop a collapse beneath it.

The first operation ends through recovery, a Well decision, and withdrawal—not extermination. That structure establishes the larger story: success is measured by what survives, what was learned, and whether the cost remains visible after the fighting stops.

Harvest

Save the present

Immediate power may keep Lume Reach alive. The Well collapses, the contained possibility is gone, and nearby terrain changes permanently.

Preserve

Keep the future open

The Well remains and provides steady value and intelligence. Holding it can extend the battle and place more lives at risk.

Reshape

Borrow another road

A possible route or cover line becomes real for a limited time. It may open an escape while exposing someone else’s flank.

There is no hidden morality meter. The campaign is designed to remember who paid, who benefited, what the player knew, and whether another option still existed.
